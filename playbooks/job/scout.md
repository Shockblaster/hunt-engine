# Scout playbook (job hunt)

## Source order and what each is good for
1. **ATS boards of watchlist companies** (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable): fresh, structured, direct apply, no account. Best signal-to-noise.
2. **Funding news** (Inc42 `https://inc42.com/feed/`, YourStory `https://yourstory.com/feed`, TechCrunch `https://techcrunch.com/feed/`): companies that just raised hire within weeks, often before posting. Check their careers page; if none, hand to referral-outreach as an open application.
3. **Wellfound** (logged in): converts well for early-career startup roles; note that you can only message a company after it opens a thread.
4. **YC Work at a Startup**: 5 applications a week; founders read them. Filter India or remote.
5. **JobSpy** (LinkedIn, Indeed, Naukri, Google Jobs): volume; many duplicates and stale posts, so check posting dates and dedupe.
6. **Internshala** for internships: check stipend and that the company is real; skip "pay for training" and commission-only.
7. **Startup maps and directories** (city startup maps that list roles and fresher-friendly flags): strong for fresher non-engineering roles.
8. **HN Who is hiring, RemoteOK, Himalayas**: remote roles; many want US hours or seniority.

## Watchlist (ATS board ids; add companies as you find them)
Greenhouse: razorpaysoftwareprivatelimited. Lever: cred. (The critic and scout grow this list; prefer companies matching the profile's sectors and stage.)

## Role queries
Built from the profile's target roles, plus common synonyms. Examples for founder's office or operations: "founder's office", "chief of staff", "business operations", "strategy and operations", "BizOps", "program manager intern", "growth associate", "operations associate", "special projects".

## Scoring reminders
- An internship or fresher role asking for 2+ years is usually eligibility 2, not 1, at startups (they often accept a test task). At big companies it is 1.
- Recency matters most on LinkedIn and Naukri (applicant pile-ups); less on founder-led startups.

## Lessons
- Aggregator dates can be stale; trust the ATS or careers page date.
- Funding reports rarely give the city; check the company.
- Check that a company's domain still belongs to it (homepage title matches the name) before anyone emails it.
